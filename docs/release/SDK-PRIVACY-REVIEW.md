The source-level privacy fixes, native Android settings check and signed-package audit are complete. This review does not establish the Apple Kids advertising exception, human content approval, physical-device acceptance or store approval.

User profiles, artwork and learning progress are not supplied to the ad SDK.

## Changes and verified safeguards

- Every ad request uses child treatment and maximum G rating before initialization, with non-personalized requests. The separate UMP under-age flag is also set; [Google notes it is not forwarded automatically to the ad SDK](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/gdpr).
- Android excludes AAID and AdServices ID/attribution/topics permissions. Location, camera, microphone, contacts and phone-state permissions are absent. Backup is disabled; cleartext traffic is disabled by the target SDK's default. iOS has no ATT prompt and delays app measurement initialization.
- Publisher first-party ID is disabled on Android through the native bridge, with persisted-setting verification; iOS uses the supported same-app-key control. Disabling these identifiers does not imply that all SDK-instance or other identifiers disappear.
- A failed or timed-out fresh UMP update now suspends ads in development and production, even if cached consent would allow requests. A failed parent-area refresh also suspends loaded ads. Four regression cases cover both platforms, cached eligibility, loaded interstitials and successful recovery.
- All 89 Flutter tests passed; analysis and the 2,400-entry catalog validation passed. The silent native Android test exercises production eligibility with official Google test ad units and isolated memory storage. It verifies native version 25.5.0, child treatment, G rating, publisher-ID disablement and no required child consent form. UMP returned `obtained` without asking for consent during this test; this is the observed SDK state, not a claim that a child granted consent. Exact network payloads were not inspected.
- The agent's emulator was restored to the normal entry point and its original airplane/Wi-Fi settings, then closed. The user's Pixel and iOS devices were not changed.

Collection is required, not claimed ephemeral; purposes are advertising, analytics/measurement and fraud prevention. The advertising-ID declaration is also saved as No, consistent with child treatment and the exact AAB permission exclusions. The live console state is recorded separately in `play-data-safety.json` and `play-child-audience.json`.

[Google's Families list](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955712?hl=en) includes Google Mobile Ads 19.0.0 or later. The pinned 25.5.0 qualifies. [Child-directed treatment](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6219315?hl=en) disables personalization, remarketing, third-party tracking pixels and advertising-ID transmission. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) permits persistent identifiers solely for internal operations/contextual ads under its stated restrictions; this is not a blanket exemption for arbitrary SDK data use.

**iOS production remains held.** [Apple Kids guideline 1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#kids-category) requires publicly documented human review of ad creatives for age appropriateness and restricted data handling. Google's Families self-certification and publisher ad review after serving do not supply that evidence. No Google support response establishing the exception was found today. The packaged Google privacy manifest still declares linked data and a generic tracking-related identifier category; it must not be altered to hide those declarations. Production iOS ads and the Kids submission cannot be enabled on the evidence available.

Human editorial/pronunciation approval (including Arabic `syllable_37.mp3`), complete physical Android/iOS and phone/tablet acceptance, and production ad-dismissal validation remain open. Signing and SDK audit success do not satisfy those gates. No store review submission or public availability is claimed.
